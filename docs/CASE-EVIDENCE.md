# Bank of Baroda, Vijaya Bank and Dena Bank: evidence review

Research cut-off: 16 October 2026. Analysis window: 31 March 2019 to 31 March 2020. Currency amounts below are INR crore. This is a historical research case, not an assessment of the bank's current condition.

**Conclusion: Too early to tell whether the merger caused improvement.** The combined gross NPA ratio declined by about 0.62 percentage points in the first year. The selected peers improved more, the available pre-trends are not parallel, and material accounting and regulatory effects remain unresolved. Selected financial facts and arithmetic are supported; a complete independently reconstructed predecessor panel is not.

## Transaction identity

The government announced the proposed combination on 17 September 2018. The statutory scheme dated 2 January 2019 transferred the entire undertakings of Vijaya Bank and Dena Bank into continuing Bank of Baroda, effective 1 April 2019. The record date was 11 March 2019. Holders received 402 and 110 Bank of Baroda shares respectively per 1,000 Vijaya and Dena shares. The scheme specifies face values of INR 2 for the new shares and INR 10 for the old shares. This is a whole-bank statutory amalgamation. Sources: [government announcement](https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/PrintRelease.aspx?lang=2&reg=3&relid=183523), [issuer notice and Gazette scheme](https://bankofbaroda.bank.in/writereaddata/Images/pdf/BOB-Amalgamation.pdf), PDF p1 and English Gazette pp8–14.

Verified continuing-bank exchange identifiers are NSE BANKBARODA and BSE 532134. The indexed BSE notice dated 28 February 2019 identifies Vijaya as 532401 and Dena as 532121; predecessor NSE tickers and ISINs remain unresolved. The first combined quarterly period ended 30 June 2019; its presentation is dated 25 July 2019.

June 2019 uses the later comparative denominator, explained below.

Recomputation from amounts gives 69,924 / 697,824 × 100 = **10.020292%**, and 69,381 / 738,096 × 100 = **9.399997%**. The change is **−0.620295 percentage points**. A symmetric two-factor decomposition, averaging numerator-first and denominator-first orderings, assigns **−0.075690 points** to the INR 543 crore numerator decline and **−0.544604 points** to the INR 40,272 crore denominator increase. These effects add exactly to the ratio change. They describe arithmetic, not merger synergies.

The [audited standalone FY2020 statement](https://bankofbaroda.bank.in/shareholders-corner/financial-reports/financial-report-2019-20) gives gross NPAs of INR 69,381.43 crore, which reconcile to the rounded presentation amount. The gross-advances denominator comes from the presentation; it is not independently certified by this review. The statutory statement's March 2019 comparative is Bank of Baroda alone, whereas March 2020 includes the amalgamated bank. Directly comparing those columns changes the perimeter.

Only the fourth post-merger quarter is below the combined March 2019 baseline. Four observations therefore do not establish four quarters of sustained improvement.

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| State Bank of India | 7.53 | 6.15 | −1.38 |
| Bank of Maharashtra | 16.40 | 12.81 | −3.59 |

Sources: [SBI FY2020 annual report, stressed assets section](https://sbi.bank.in/corporate/AR1920/download_center/english/11-3.16-Stressed%20Assets%20Management.pdf), printed p65; [Bank of Maharashtra FY2020 results](https://bankofmaharashtra.bank.in/writereaddata/documentlibrary/02b22ec5-3d88-4136-81a8-c394bfa6d687.pdf), p3, dated 16 June 2020.

Neither peer undertook a new whole-bank amalgamation in this analysis window. SBI is much larger and had an earlier 2017 merger; Maharashtra is smaller, predominantly domestic and starts with much worse asset quality. These are descriptive comparators, not exchangeable controls. The median individual peer change is **−2.485 points**. The combined bank's relative change is therefore **+1.864705 points**, adverse for this lower-is-better metric. Choosing only SBI or only Maharashtra gives adverse gaps of approximately +0.76 or +2.97 points.

SBI's June 2018, September 2018 and March 2019 ratios were 10.69%, 9.95% and 7.53% ([FY2019 analyst presentation](https://sbi.bank.in/documents/17836/18638/100519-Analyst%2BPPT.pdf), p32). The corresponding combined-bank minus SBI gaps widen from 1.69 to 1.90 to 2.49 points. This fails a simple parallel-pre-trend diagnostic. Maharashtra has only one matching pre-period in this evidence set. Synthetic-control weights and causal effects are unavailable.

For display, an exact descriptive bridge can begin with acquirer-only reported GNPA of 9.61%: perimeter effect **+0.410292**, peer movement **−2.485000**, and remaining difference **+1.864705**, reaching 9.399997%. The remainder is **unexplained**, not synergy. Accounting adjustments and one-offs are unquantified, not assumed zero. Do not add the separate numerator/denominator decomposition to this bridge: it explains the same ratio change in another way.

## Other metrics and counterevidence

The supplied JSON distinguishes amount-supported comparisons from display-only ratios and missing data. Net NPA, global CASA and cost/income have underlying baseline and post amounts; their full predecessor, adjustment and causal reconciliation remains incomplete. For example, annual cost/income recomputes to 17,928 / (25,678 + 8,794) = **52.0074%**, and 18,077.19 / (75,983.65 − 48,532.37 + 10,317.33) = **47.8630%**. The latter inputs use the audited statement, converted from INR lakh to crore. The issuer identifies prior-year amalgamation one-offs and treasury-income changes, so this decline is not a verified operating synergy.

NIM, ROA, credit cost, slippages, provision coverage, cost of deposits, regulatory capital and basic EPS retain selected reported figures only. Average earning assets, average equity, risk-weighted assets, share counts and comparable provision scope are not sufficiently verified to rebuild those ratios. ROE, full cost of funds and book value per share are unsupported; missing values stay missing. Cost of deposits is not a substitute for cost of funds. Global CASA is not domestic CASA.

FY2020 audited net profit was INR 546.18 crore, despite a pretax loss of INR 1,802.11 crore, because of a tax credit of INR 2,348.29 crore. Positive net profit consequently does not establish operating improvement. Capital issuance, COVID-related regulatory measures and incomplete integration are additional unresolved attribution issues.

Management's original benefits were qualitative. No verified numerical rupee synergy target was found. Reported branch and ATM rationalization and partial technology migration are operational milestones, not audited savings. The comparison therefore cannot be scored as a delivered quantitative synergy promise.

## Revisions, quarantines and data discipline

- The initial RBI gross-NPA divergence of INR 5,250 crore was superseded by **INR 2,048 crore** in the [FY2020 annual report](https://www.bseindia.com/bseplus/AnnualReport/532134/5321340320.pdf), table “Divergence in Asset Classification and Provisioning of NPAs.” Reported GNPA of 69,924 becomes assessed GNPA of 71,972. Holding the denominator fixed increases the baseline by 0.293484 points. This is a separate sensitivity, never an additive synergy adjustment. The earlier [Q3 disclosure](https://links.sgx.com/1.0.0/corporate-announcements/52MIC78N61SZJHY4/594561_SGXFinal.pdf), note20, is retained as superseded. The annual table was verified through indexed official-source extraction; full-PDF retrieval and its exact page remain unverified.
- June 2019 gross advances are 678,322 in Q1 p42 and 678,142 in Q2 p40. The later comparative is selected; the old value remains quarantined.
- Opening combined cost/income of 53.19% in Q1 p6 conflicts with 52.01% and supporting annual amounts in Q4. The latter is selected, without inventing an explanation.
- A Dena GNPA ratio of 10.91% in the Q1 table is inconsistent with the surrounding combination evidence and is not used.
- Q4 p34 contains conflicting quarterly and annual NPA-reduction subcategories. No recovery/write-off flow waterfall is built from those values.

## Evidence boundaries and next requirements

The JSON contains 114 observations and 13 source records, each observation linked to a source and locator. Source records distinguish known publication dates from unknown dates and describe extraction methods. No crawl date substitutes for a publication date. Publicly accessible official issuer, government and exchange sources were used; no access controls were bypassed, and source PDFs are not redistributed.

Final source checks added annual global NIM of 2.64% for combined FY2019 and 2.73% for FY2020 from the yearly chart on presentation page 28. These remain reported-only. The Q2 cover and NSE filing establish 8 November 2019; the Q4 presentation is recorded at its 24 June 2020 exchange filing date. The dated issuer results release establishes 23 June 2020 for the results announcement. Unknown annual-report publication dates remain unknown. The June 2019 results notes disclose pooling of interests under AS 14; no acquisition-accounting adjustment is presumed.

The available GNPA history has three combined pre-period observations and four post quarters, not an 8–12-quarter predecessor panel. Before a stronger verdict, obtain each predecessor's original GNPA and gross-advances schedule; reconcile eliminations and accounting policies; resolve revisions and regulatory relief; expand prehistory and peer checks; and observe sustained improvement after integration. These are outstanding evidence requirements, not zero-valued adjustments.
